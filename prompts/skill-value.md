# Skill: seen, remembered, believed, bought

A hook that only stops the thumb buys one view. A channel is built on the
four things a viewer does in order, and every title, caption, description
and pinned comment you write should do at least one of them on purpose:

| stage | what it means here | what you write |
|---|---|---|
| **Seen** | the thumb stops | the first three words ask them to pick |
| **Remembered** | they recognise the next clip as ours | the same ritual, the same words, every time |
| **Believed** | they trust the result was not rigged | honest scene, real numbers, never a claim we cannot show |
| **Bought** | they act: comment, follow, come back | one ask, said once, at the moment it costs nothing |

## Seen — the pick, nothing else

Covered in the hooks skill: lead with the pick, name the stage, no result,
no spec sheet, no colour list. One addition: **specific beats loud.** "Pick
one — five go down the plinko" is stopped by more thumbs than "INSANE marble
race" — the count and the stage give the viewer something to do, the
adjective gives them nothing. Never use a superlative the clip does not earn
on screen.

## Remembered — a ritual the viewer can name

A channel is remembered for a thing it does the same way each time. Ours:

- **The ask is always the same shape.** Caption: two or three words, second
  person, a verb (`PICK ONE`, `CALL IT NOW`). Title: the pick, the count,
  the stage. Pinned comment: the colours, "which did you back?" A viewer who has
  seen three of our clips should be able to say the caption before it
  appears.
- **The lineup is always colours, named in the same order as on screen**,
  left to right — in the pinned comment and the description, where the
  viewer answers with one. Not "the fast one", not "the underdog" — those
  are results wearing a disguise, and they are not repeatable.
- **The stage is a name, not a number.** "on the drums", "in the funnels",
  "in the arena". A viewer remembers *the drums one*; nobody remembers
  *the 31-bumper one*.
- **Series words are allowed and wanted:** "Run it back", "Round 2",
  "Rematch". They tell the viewer this is a thing that keeps happening,
  which is the whole reason to follow.

Do not invent a new ritual per clip. Variety lives in the stage, the
colours and the physics; the words are the constant.

## Believed — nothing we cannot show

The result is real physics from a seed, and the viewer can feel it when
the words match what they see. Trust is lost in one clip and not regained:

- **The description's first sentence is the scene in the present tense**,
  exactly as rendered (the facts say how many marbles, which stage, how
  long). No invented drama, no "the closest finish ever" unless the margin
  in facts is the smallest this channel has logged.
- **Never imply a rigged or scripted outcome** and never suggest one
  colour is favoured. "Red never loses" is a lie one seed in three and
  reads as a setup on the seed it is true.
- **Say the boring true thing rather than the exciting false one.** "Same
  three marbles, new stage" is believed; "This one broke the simulation"
  is not.
- **If the render shows a flaw** (a marble stalls, a gate clips), the facts
  will say so; do not title around it. Reject or re-render instead.

## Bought — one ask, once, when it is free

"Buying" on this channel is a comment, a follow, and coming back for the
next one. The ask works when it costs the viewer nothing and comes after
they have something to say:

- **On screen, after the result** (the render does this): `COMMENT YOUR
  PICK`. It is not in the title and not in the caption, where it would
  compete with the pick.
- **The pinned comment is the ask written out**: "Red, blue or amber —
  which did you back?" A question they can answer in one word. Never two
  asks in one comment; "comment AND subscribe AND share" is three asks and
  gets none.
- **The description's last line may name the series**: "Next race: same
  marbles, the drums." A reason to come back that is also true, because a
  queued task can make it so.
- **Never bribe** ("subscribe for a shoutout"), never guilt ("only 2% of
  you are subscribed") — both are read as weakness and neither is ours.

## The order matters

If a line can do only one of these, do the one earlier in the list: a
viewer who is not stopped is not remembered, one who does not remember is
not convinced, and one who is not convinced does not act. So the title is
for being seen, the caption for being seen and remembered, the description
for being believed, and the pinned comment for being bought.
